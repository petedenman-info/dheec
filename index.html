Where we stand

The timeline moved, and the fall is ours

For the fourth long session the coalition will introduce legislation removing the income and asset limits in the EPD (Employed People with Disabilities) program, so workers with disabilities can earn and save while keeping Medicaid coverage for health care and LTSS (long term services and supports). The language is unchanged from SB 20 in 2025.

The reset

September 11 was never the deadline

That date applied only to getting concepts to Legislative Counsel, and ours was delivered in July. The date that binds is the early filing drop in December, with signed sponsors. The fall is available for recruiting sponsors, endorsements, and fiscal groundwork.

The asset

Chief sponsors in both chambers

Two in the Senate and two in the House, plus one additional signed sponsor. Our House chief sponsor is Vice Chair of Ways and Means and has said he will make the bill a top Republican budget priority in the committee that held it in 2025.

The open question

One bill or two

Our Senate carrier does not want parallel House and Senate versions and reports that Senate leadership agrees, on the grounds that duelling identical bills cause confusion and create barriers. The August two version decision is up for revisiting on October 7.

On the fiscal estimate. The fiscal impact remains the central obstacle, and there is relevant history on how the 2025 estimate was arrived at. That background is in the emailed status report of September 18 rather than on this page. The forward plan is in the open decisions below.

Closed

Settled since the September 2 meeting

  • The September 11 deadline. Resolved as a non-issue; the concept reached Legislative Counsel in July.
  • The House filing question. Superseded by the one bill guidance, pending the Executive Committee decision on October 7.
  • The employer sponsored insurance (ESI) question. Decided. The bill keeps the same language as SB 20 with no ESI requirement, after the coalition considered and set aside a requirement that participants carry their own or a spouse's employer coverage.
  • Second House chief sponsor. Confirmed, and named as chief sponsor in the coalition's external updates.
  • Meeting with our Senate carrier. Held September 3 with the Senator and her legislative assistant. A fuller meeting with coalition leadership will be scheduled through that office, and her assistant has offered to attend coalition meetings during the session.
  • 501(c)(4) filing. Federal Form 8976, the notice of intent to operate under Section 501(c)(4) as the Oregon Disability Equity Action, filed and confirmed September 3.
  • HSCO endorsement application. The application for the Concentrated Advocacy Endorsement of the Human Services Coalition of Oregon was submitted September 16, on our Senate carrier's recommendation. The outcome is pending.
  • Draft legislation copy. Circulated September 16 for use when seeking support.
  • City of Portland. The City's Office of Government Relations received a full status update on September 16 and replied the same day that the City has supported the concept before and would be unlikely not to support it again. The City's 2027 agenda setting is still in progress.
  • Meeting cadence. Monthly Executive Committee, 5:30 p.m. start. Next meeting October 7.

Open decisions

Suggested agenda for October 7

Each of these is a decision or follow-up the email record leaves open. The December drop date means none is urgent, and all are cheaper to settle now than in January.

  1. Decide one bill or two

    Our Senate carrier and Senate leadership want one. The August decision to pursue both a Senate and a House version is up for revisiting. Amendment strategy belongs in the same discussion, including whether to hold an employer sponsored insurance amendment in reserve in case it becomes necessary to move the bill.

    Owner Executive Committee  ·  Decide Oct 7

  2. Adopt the fiscal plan

    The proposal on the table is to identify, before the session starts, who the go-to analyst is at the Legislative Fiscal Office and who defines the underlying assumptions at ODHS, and to engage both early rather than reacting to a number once it lands. Also to be decided: whether to request the revised 2025 fiscal document so its assumptions can be examined before they circulate again.

    Owner Unassigned  ·  Decide Oct 7

  3. Approve the asks for the leadership meeting

    Four commitments have been proposed for the meeting with our Senate carrier and coalition leadership: that she not stop without the coalition's agreement, that she alert the coalition immediately if the bill comes under pressure from an agency, that the coalition obtain the revised 2025 fiscal document, and that the bill be placed on the Senate priority list to match the commitment we have in the House. No objections have been raised in the thread.

    Owner Executive Committee  ·  Meeting To be scheduled

  4. Send the draft to Rep. Nelson and Minority Leader Elmer

    Both conditioned their interest on seeing a draft, and the copy circulated September 16 satisfies that. Rep. Nelson also asked in August who was submitting the Legislative Counsel request and whether we were working with the same sponsors as before; those questions have not been answered. Assign who sends it.

    Owner Unassigned  ·  Effort One email each

    The one to two page handout needs refreshing alongside the circulated draft. The comparison state list should carry the corrected set: Washington, Hawaii, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, and Rhode Island.

    Owner Unassigned

    The August 21 letter names one senator, went to a similarly spelled senator's address, and a third spelling appears in the September 16 update. Resolve which legislator is intended before following up.

    A standing invitation to agency leadership to attend a coalition meeting has been open since June. It predates what we learned on September 3, and the committee should decide whether it stands, changes, or is withdrawn. Background is in the emailed report.

Where we stand

The draft came back, and it is not quite our bill

For the fourth long session the coalition will introduce legislation reforming the EPD (Employed People with Disabilities) program so workers with disabilities can earn and save while keeping Medicaid coverage for health care and LTSS (long term services and supports). Legislative Counsel has returned a draft, numbered LC 1283, and it differs from the concept we submitted. That difference is the live question.

Needs a decision

A 600 percent FPL cap appeared in the draft

Our concept removed the income and asset limits. The returned draft sets an income limit at 600 percent of the FPL (Federal Poverty Level). The view on the table is that this is a workable compromise if it becomes necessary to pass the bill, but premature to concede now, and that it should be carried as a Dash-1 amendment that can be dropped.

Waiting on the committee

Feedback on LC 1283 is overdue

The marked-up draft went to the Executive Committee on November 7 with a request for feedback or approval before it goes back for finalization. It was chased again on November 9. One member has responded. The draft does not move until the rest of us weigh in.

Regrouping

The full coalition reconvenes November 18

Agreed at the November 4 Executive meeting. Kickoff is 5:30 p.m. on Zoom, with dedicated speaking time for legislators, and sponsors and recruits being invited. The agenda is still open and input was requested.

On the fiscal estimate. The fiscal impact remains the central obstacle, and there is relevant history on how the 2025 estimate was arrived at. That background is in the emailed status reports rather than on this page. The forward plan is in the open decisions below, and it is still unowned.

The draft

Where LC 1283 sits and what is in dispute

  • The number is a draft number, not a bill number. LC 1283 is the Legislative Counsel draft. Nothing has been filed and no bill number has been assigned.
  • We have a copy. Our Senate carrier provided it on November 3, earlier than the usual turnaround, after a direct request. A marked-up version with tracked changes and margin comments followed on November 7.
  • Some of the changes help and some do not. The assessment circulated with the draft is that certain edits strengthened our intent while others may work against it, which is why the committee is being asked to pick through them rather than simply approve.
  • The 600 percent FPL income limit is the substantive one. Everything else in the markup is smaller. The proposal on the table is to accept it only as a Dash-1 amendment held in reserve.
  • Turnaround time is unknown. Asked directly on November 4 how long we have to return edits, the answer was that nobody knows. That argues for returning them quickly rather than waiting to find out.

Two amendment ideas are in play alongside the draft. A participant fee tier, which would keep people on EPD above 250 percent FPL in exchange for a monthly fee set as a percentage of gross income, with the revenue earmarked for EPD administration and growth. And a dedicated fund capturing income tax revenue generated by EPD participants, modeled on an existing Oregon special fund. Both are detailed in the October email thread; neither has been adopted.

No follow-up has been reported since.

Open decisions

What needs answering, most urgent first

The first item has a clock on it. The rest should be settled before the December drop.

  1. Return feedback on LC 1283

    The marked-up draft went out November 7 asking for feedback or approval before it goes back to our Senate carrier for finalization, and was chased on November 9 as needed right away. One member has responded. Everyone else owes a reply, and the turnaround we have is unknown, so this is the item that holds up the bill.

    Owner Every Exec member  ·  Status Overdue

  2. Decide the 600 percent FPL cap

    Accept it, strike it, or carry it as a Dash-1 amendment that can be dropped if it is not needed. The recommendation on the table is the third. This is the one substantive policy change in the returned draft and it decides what the bill actually does.

    Owner Executive Committee  ·  Decide With the LC 1283 feedback

  3. Assign the fiscal plan

    Identify who the go-to analyst is at the Legislative Fiscal Office and who defines the underlying assumptions at ODHS, and engage both before session rather than reacting to a number once it lands. This has been open since September and nobody owns it. Two fiscal questions now sit on top of it: whether participant fees can count as revenue, and whether a dedicated fund can be made to work when the tax revenue generated may not fully offset the General Fund investment.

    Owner Unassigned  ·  Open since September

  4. Settle the participant fee proposal

    The idea is a fee tier that keeps people on EPD above 250 percent FPL in exchange for a monthly percentage of gross income, earmarked for EPD administration and growth. The structural feedback was that a legislative concept cannot amend an administrative rule directly, so the bill would need to direct the agency to change the rule through statute, and that the existing fee subsection should be modified rather than a new one added, to avoid creating a gap that catches almost nobody. Nothing has been decided and it is not clear whether any version of this reached the draft.

    Owner Executive Committee  ·  Status Unresolved

    Agenda items were requested so the meeting packet can go to the full roster. Only the legislator speaking slot is confirmed. The packet has not gone out yet and the meeting is close.

Oregon Disability Health & Employment Equity Coalition

Work should not cost you your health care.

DHEEC is a statewide coalition working to remove the income and asset limits that force Oregonians with disabilities to choose between a career and the care that keeps them alive.

The problem

Most people with disabilities want to work. Work brings community, purpose, and dignity, the same as it does for everyone else. Oregon's EPD program, Employed Persons with Disabilities, is a Medicaid work incentive meant to make that possible: it lets a person with a disability hold a job while keeping Medicaid coverage for health care and for long term services and supports, the daily in-home care that many people need to get up in the morning and into bed at night.

But the program's income and asset limits have not kept up. Under the current rules, a raise, a promotion, or ordinary retirement savings can put a person's eligibility at risk. People turn down advancement, cap their own careers, and keep their assets below limits set decades ago, because losing Medicaid can mean losing the care that makes independent life possible.

The result is a system where the penalty for succeeding at work is losing the support that made work possible.

The legislation

DHEEC has led the coalition effort behind legislation that removes the income and asset limits for EPD eligibility. In 2025 it was Senate Bill 20: a bill directing the state to provide medical assistance to employed individuals with disabilities regardless of income or resources, with income still considered for cost sharing, so participants contribute as they earn more. The same reform returns in the 2027 session.

  • Employment without a cliff.People keep Medicaid coverage for health care and long term services and supports while they work, earn, and advance.
  • A fair contribution, not a trap.Income can still be considered for cost sharing. The bill replaces an all-or-nothing cutoff with a path where working more never means losing care.
  • Lower long-term public cost.People who can work and keep their benefits rely less on other state and federal programs, are healthier, and contribute to the tax base.

Several states have already removed income and resource limits for workers with disabilities:

  • Washington
  • Hawaii
  • Massachusetts
  • Minnesota
  • New Jersey
  • Rhode Island

Where things stand

The 2027 bill keeps the same language as SB 20. The coalition spent 2026 securing legislative sponsors, endorsements, and partner support, and the bill is on track for early filing ahead of the session that convenes in January 2027.

Who we are

The Oregon Disability Health and Employment Equity Coalition brings together people with disabilities, their families, care providers, and advocacy organizations from across the state. Our mission is to make Medicaid, health care, and employment equitable for people with disabilities in Oregon.

Coalition participants include Disability Rights Oregon, the Oregon State Independent Living Council, the Oregon Disabilities Commission, Disability Services Advisory Councils, the Oregon State Rehabilitation Council, SEIU, the Campaign for Oregon's Seniors and People with Disabilities, Oregon Spinal Cord Injury Connection, the Oregon Consumer League, the Oregon Health Care Association, the Oregon State Council for Retired Citizens, Oregon Self Advocates, and others.
